Optimal Multiharmonic Forcing Design for Homoclinic Chaos Control via Convex Optimization

Víctor M. Preciado

University of Pennsylvania

Read the paper (PDF) Code and data on GitHub Online Resource 1 (ZIP)

Abstract

Small periodic perturbations can split a homoclinic orbit and produce transverse intersections between the stable and unstable manifolds of a saddle. We design phase-locked multiharmonic forcing to prevent these crossings near a specified orbit for sufficiently small perturbations. The design maximizes the uniform distance of the first-order Melnikov function from zero under a power budget, or minimizes the power required for a prescribed margin. For a finite harmonic drive, the Melnikov function is a trigonometric polynomial whose coefficients depend affinely on the sine and cosine control amplitudes. The Fejér–Riesz theorem gives an exact semidefinite certificate for its sign over all forcing phases. In a Helmholtz escape oscillator, we prove that control at the fundamental frequency has a sharp margin ceiling of approximately 0.0355 at any power. Adding the second harmonic makes a margin of 0.08 attainable. For a Duffing resonator and a Josephson junction, multiharmonic control reduces power by 27% and 7%, respectively, at matched margins certified on both homoclinic branches. Nonlinear simulations assess the finite-perturbation response through manifold tracing, periodic-orbit diagnostics, and finite-horizon escape tests.
